---
title: "What an AI governance audit covers"
publishedAt: "2026-08-31"
summary: "An overview of the controls, evidence, and readiness questions in an AI governance audit framework."
---

An AI governance audit examines how an organization moves from unmanaged experimentation toward secure, auditable, sovereignty-aligned AI adoption. Its role is to support alignment and operational readiness, not to promise compliance or issue a certification.

The framework uses one controls dataset as the shared source for the document kit and scoring tool. That shared spine connects methodology, questionnaires, evidence templates, and reporting to the same set of controls rather than letting separate deliverables drift.

Coverage spans eight domains: governance, privacy and data, cybersecurity, vendor risk, sovereignty, operational risk, workforce readiness, and transparency and auditability. Controls are rated on a zero-to-five scale, then rolled into domain results and an overall maturity score with named levels.

The audit can follow two paths. A greenfield pathway addresses organizations that have not yet adopted AI, while an exposure-and-gap pathway assesses systems already in use and identifies remediation work. A curated rapid subset provides a focused entry point before a full assessment.

Evidence matters as much as scoring. The framework pairs controls with document and report templates, identifies lower-scoring controls for remediation, and uses an isolated sandbox with synthetic data to demonstrate safe validation before production. Its outputs are readiness evidence, not a pass/fail certification.
